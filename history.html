Lebanon`s 1943 Independence

On the night of November 21, 1943, Lebanese political leaders, including President Bechara El Khoury and Prime Minister Riad Al Solh, were held in the Rashaya Citadel, but the mounting internal and international pressure made their release imminent. Following an ultimatum from the British to the French authorities, the detainees were released on the morning of November 22, 1943, which is now celebrated as Lebanese Independence Day.

Here are the key events leading up to and during that time:

  • The Arrest (Nov 11, 1943): Following a parliamentary vote to amend the constitution and remove all references to the French Mandate, French authorities arrested President Bechara El Khoury, Prime Minister Riad Al Solh, and other officials (Camille Chamoun, Adel Osseiran, Abdel Hamid Karameh, and Selim Takla).
  • The Detention (Rashaya): They were imprisoned in the Rashaya Citadel in the Bekaa Valley, which was nicknamed the "Citadel of Independence" or "Fort of November 22".
  • The Protests & Interim Government: Massive popular demonstrations, bringing together both Christian and Muslim communities, broke out across Lebanon, and a provisional government was established in Bchamoun.
  • International Pressure: On November 21, 1943, the British threatened to intervene militarily, threatening a "deadline" of 10:00 AM on November 22, 1943, for the release of the prisoners.
  • Release and Independence (Nov 22, 1943): France surrendered to the pressure, releasing the prisoners before noon on November 22. The leaders returned to Beirut, the French flag was lowered, and the Lebanese flag was raised, marking the true, functional independence of the country.

This event represents a moment of national unity that forced the French to officially recognize Lebanese sovereignty and end the Mandate, with the National Pact established shortly after to govern the new state.
